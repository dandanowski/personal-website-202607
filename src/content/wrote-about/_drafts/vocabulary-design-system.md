A design system without shared language is just a component library with a wiki. The vocabulary is what makes the decisions legible — to the people who built it and to the people who inherit it.

<!-- excerpt -->

---

* Naming is a design decision, not a documentation task — it happens at the point of creating the component or pattern, not after
* A shared vocabulary creates alignment without meetings — if everyone calls it the same thing, fewer conversations are needed to get on the same page
* Bad names encode bad decisions — a component called "card" that does five different things means the system hasn't resolved what those five things actually are
* The vocabulary reflects the model — if the names are confusing, the underlying mental model probably is too
* Consistency matters more than perfection — a slightly wrong name used consistently beats a perfectly correct name used differently across teams
